# Why Biomedical Engineering → Product Management Makes Sense

> **Week:** 2024-W01 | **Category:** Career

---

## Topic

Why my biomedical engineering background is not a detour into PM — it is a direct path.

---

## Summary

Most people assume that PM requires a computer science or business background. The more I've studied what great PMs actually do, the more I believe that engineering systems thinking — which biomedical engineering trains deeply — is exactly the right foundation. The body is the most complex system humans have ever tried to understand. Products are systems too.

---

## Key Concepts

1. **Systems thinking is the core PM skill.** PMs must understand how changes to one part of a system (a feature, a metric, a price) cascade through the rest. Biomedical engineers are trained to think in systems: feedback loops, failure modes, emergent behaviour.

2. **User research is clinical observation with different vocabulary.** Medical professionals observe patients in their environment, interview them about their experience, and form hypotheses. PMs do the same. The methods are nearly identical.

3. **Constraint-based design is the Kenyan PM reality.** In medical device design, you work within tight safety, cost, and regulatory constraints. In Kenyan product design, you work within connectivity, device, and affordability constraints. The discipline of designing under constraints is the same.

4. **Domain expertise creates unfair advantage.** Most PM candidates study generic case studies. I can tear down M-Tiba, Ilara Health, and hospital management systems with genuine domain knowledge. That is not a minor differentiator — in healthtech hiring, it is decisive.

5. **The evidence base matters.** Medical training instils respect for evidence before decision-making. This is the right instinct for product analytics, user research, and PRD writing.

---

## Why It Matters for PMs

A PM without systems thinking reacts to individual tickets. A PM with systems thinking understands how those tickets connect to user outcomes, business metrics, and long-term product architecture. Biomedical engineering gave me the habit of thinking systemically before acting.

---

## How I'm Applying It

I'm leading with the healthtech angle in every portfolio project. The hospital queue management case study is not a generic exercise — it's me applying domain expertise to a real, observable problem. The medical equipment maintenance tracker does the same. I'm building a portfolio that only I could build.
